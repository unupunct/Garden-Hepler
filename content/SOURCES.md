# Content provenance

**Status: starter content, not yet individually reviewed against primary sources.**

The plant records, companion relationships and rotation rules were compiled by the project from widely published horticultural guidance (extension services, botanical-garden and society advice). Each record says so in its `sources` entry, carries a `confidence` and a `reviewed` date, and none of them cites a specific publication it does not actually draw on.

- Figures (spacing, days to maturity, sowing weeks) are **typical ranges for temperate climates**. Verify against your local extension service before relying on them.
- Companion claims are labelled by evidence: *research*, *recommendation*, *traditional* or *anecdotal*. Several well-known folk pairings are deliberately marked anecdotal or uncertain.
- Rotation periods are general guidance; crop- and site-specific disease history may call for longer breaks.
- No text or images are copied from any publication.

To raise the quality of a record, add real citations (title, organization, URL, access date) to its `sources` list and update `reviewed` and `confidence`.

## Romanian wild and useful plants, medicinal plants, wild mushrooms (2026-10-29)

Files: `ro-pollinators.json`, `ro-pest-soil.json`, `ro-medicinal.json`, `ro-wild-mushrooms.json`, `ro-trees-shrubs.json`, plus origin, season, benefits and medicinal additions to older records.

Status: compiled from standard European botanical, gardening and herbal references (for example Flora Europaea, Flora of Romania, RHS and extension guidance; the EMA herbal monographs for many medicinal plants). Confidence is `moderate`. Romanian common names are the widely used ones; where no common name is well established a descriptive name or the Latin name is used. **Medicinal and mushroom entries must be reviewed by a qualified person (botanist, pharmacist or authorised mushroom expert) before the app is promoted as a reference.** Mushroom entries support recognition and safety awareness only and say so.
